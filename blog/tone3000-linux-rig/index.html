From Wine to GuitarMood: A Free, Native Linux Guitar Rig Anyone Can Run

Part two: my FCB1010 now drives a free, open-source guitar rig on Omarchy. Seven eras of Iron Maiden, and GuitarMood, an app that is the pedalboard: open it to play, close it to stop.▋

Part two. Part one: How Claude's Fable Model Saved My Gear and Got Me Excited About Playing Guitar Again

GuitarMood, a real screenshot with the rig live: bank 3, RHYTHM lit, the song's key, tempo and solo echo up top.

TL;DR

Last time, my FCB1010 finally drove BIAS FX 2. Now it drives a guitar rig that runs natively on Linux, where every piece of software is free and open source. GuitarMood is its face: open it from the Omarchy launcher and the whole rig starts; the window is the pedalboard, live and two-way; close it and everything saves and shuts down. It tiles, it autoscales, and it wears your Omarchy theme.

A 10-minute read. Every experiment, chart and preset behind it is in the lab notes. Code: github.com/saada/fcb1010-biasfx2 (MIT).

Act one · leaving Wine

10 ms on paper, mud in my hands

Last time, the pedalboard that lived under my desk for years finally talked to BIAS FX 2, and I stood in my living room grinning through The Evil That Men Do. Then I did the thing every developer threatens to do: I left macOS for Omarchy, the opinionated Arch + Hyprland setup. I love it. My guitar rig did not come along quietly.

BIAS FX 2 is Windows/macOS only, so it came along under Wine. The scripts from last time still worked, presets loaded, the pedal switched them, and the app claimed about 10 ms of latency. It felt like playing through a swimming pool. That number only covers the app's own buffer; under Wine the audio also crosses an ASIO shim, a JACK bridge and PipeWire, and none of that shows up in the friendly "10 ms". Skill issue? Maybe. Architecture issue? Definitely.

A native amp sim, and presets as code

Then TONE3000 shipped a native Linux plugin. TONE3000 is a huge, free, community catalog of Neural Amp Modeler captures: real amps, pedals and whole rigs, modelled until the model is basically the box, plus impulse responses for cabinets, rooms and plates. A 1972 Hiwatt. The Gallien-Krueger preamp from Adrian Smith's Seventh Son rack. The player is open source under MIT, and I was not ready for how good it sounds. At a 256-sample buffer it felt tighter than BIAS under Wine at a nominal 128.

It has no scripting API, but it has files. A preset is a binary JUCE ValueTree that embeds the tone data itself, and Program Change N loads the Nth entry of a JSON list. Round-trip those and a script can write presets the app can't tell from hand-made ones. Then I pointed Claude Code at the catalog with fifteen research agents, one per band, each finding the best-era rig, citing sources and writing the preset as JSON. My favourite find: Iron Maiden went solid-state Gallien-Krueger for Somewhere in Time and Seventh Son, not Marshall. I had no idea.

Two things I only learned by measuring: 256 samples with multi-core on meant zero xruns, and a Linux volume slider had been quietly stealing 24 dB between the rig and my ears. (The whole act, with charts →)

Act two · a DAW nobody clicks

Generated, never clicked

A standalone amp sim can't do a real wah, a harmony or a compressor. A DAW can, but DAWs want you to click. So the DAW is generated: qtractor_rig.py writes the entire Qtractor session (tracks, plugins, MIDI bindings) and starts it. Nothing in it was ever touched with a mouse. A compressor and limiter on the way out cut the loudness spread across presets from 9.2 to 4.6 dB and ended a day of hand-levelling. (Picking the DAW, and whether the compressor earned its place →)

Act three · up the Irons

Seven eras of Maiden on one floorboard

Then I asked for the fun part: every Iron Maiden era from The Number of the Beast to Brave New World, with rhythm, solo, clean and acoustic sounds I can switch mid-song, as close to the records as possible. Seven more research agents, one per album, wrote 21 presets backed by 143 source citations. Every bank is one era, and every bank has the same board:

  • Scenes switch without a gap. A preset load leaves a 60–170 ms hole in the sound, a skipped beat in The Trooper. So each era runs a heavy and a clean rig side by side, and the scene switches send absolute CCs that re-route the guitar and push the drive. No preset load, no hole.
  • The second guitarist is a footswitch again. Last time's 3rds harmonizer was a BIAS FX 2 trick, and TONE3000 has none. So HARMONY (SW7) now plays a diatonic third above whatever I play, in the song's key, through the partner's own amp: Smith or Gers for that era. It's built from x42 autotune stages on the clean signal, and the pitch test put 8 of 8 notes on the right third.
  • Every song brings its own echo. SOLO pushes the amp, adds 2 dB and a lead echo timed to that era's signature song: 375 ms for The Trooper, 750 ms for Fear of the Dark.

I played it live from the real board, every switch's MIDI checked on the way in, and my verdict was "sounds amazing". (The seven eras, the gear, the harmony and every measurement →)

Act four · five tuners

SW10: five tries at a tuner

The last switch should have been the easy one. TONE3000's own tuner is a button with no MIDI, so SW10 mutes the rig and opens a tuner on the guitar instead. Picking that tuner went sideways:

  1. Hand-rolled in Python. No: I wanted existing software.
  2. x42 Tuna via jalv. Needed XWayland. Worked. Ugly.
  3. FMIT. Fixed Qt layouts that tiling crushed. My review, verbatim: "insanely ugly with bad aspect ratios".
  4. Chromatic. Beautiful, after a Hyprland window rule. Then practice started cutting up, and I typed "oh i think it's picking up from my mic?" It was: two leftover instances were listening to the mic, not the guitar, and cost the rig about 250 dropouts in 12 seconds. Fixed, but it took a port re-patcher and a kill-before-open rule to show one note name.
  5. Stop building, go shopping. Then it hit me: "all our tuners are dumb solutions... omarchy has existing plugins in the marketplace".

It does. Fretwise (MIT, verified in the Omarchy marketplace) lives in the bar, draws the note, the cents and the string in the theme's colours, and only listens while its panel is open. SW10 points it at the guitar input and opens its panel through the shell's IPC; the next press hides it. No window to float, nothing left running. Lesson learned: before building the thing, check what your desktop already ships. (All five, with screenshots →)

Act five · GuitarMood

GuitarMood: the rig as an Omarchy app

Last time I ended with a promise: a TUI that draws the pedalboard and lights up the switch you just stomped. By now the rig sounded right, but using it still meant a terminal to start it and a pedal that tells you nothing. The FCB1010 shows a bank number and one LED. Is the wah on? Did the boost reset when I changed song? Which bank was Fear of the Dark? So I asked for "a desktop app that shows live status of what i selected in the pedal as a hypland tile and autoscale and plays nice with omarchy... I don't have to guess anything."

Claude's first plan was GTK. I asked: "omarchy seems to love Qt?" It does: the Omarchy shell is Quickshell, which is QML. So the promised TUI grew into a Qt window instead: GuitarMood, a uv project with a QML front end. (Editing the layout from it is still to come; the board itself is still two tables in rig.py.) It reads the active theme's colours and recolours live when I switch themes.

The window is the pedalboard

The layout mirrors the FCB1010 under my feet: toggles on top, scenes below, UP and DOWN naming the bank one tap away, expression pedals on the right, the setlist down the side. Every tile says what that switch does in the bank I'm on. Everything is sized from the window, so it fits whatever tile Hyprland hands it. In a quarter of the screen it still reads from across the room:

A quarter tile, sharing the screen with btop, pw-top and a MIDI monitor (tap to enlarge).

And Omarchy's pop-out (SUPER+O) pins it to every workspace. Below about 700 px wide it switches to a compact layout: the bank, the scene in huge letters, and the toggles as chips. A status light for the rig that follows me around:

Popped out with SUPER+O: bank 3, RHYTHM, five toggles, none on.

Never guess

The pedal only sends fixed messages (a toggle sends the same CC 127 whether it's switching on or off), so GuitarMood rebuilds the state from the MIDI, following the rig's own rules. DAW effects (wah, harmony) survive a song change; amp-sim blocks (boost, delay) reset to the preset's own state on every preset load; the scene is read from the message that ends each switch's burst. If it joins a rig that's already running, it reads what it can from the saved session and shows "?" for the rest until you press something. The tests replay the MIDI of the first real session I played and check that it ends exactly where I did.

On is open, off is close, and it's two-way

Open it from the launcher and the rig starts, with Qtractor parked on a hidden workspace. A second launch just focuses the window. Close it (SUPER+W), log out or Ctrl-C, and it closes the tuner, saves the session and quits Qtractor; tested end to end, nothing left running. And it works both ways: click a tile or press 1–9, 0 and GuitarMood sends exactly what the FCB would, from the same code that wrote the pedal's layout. The pedal itself can't be told anything, so its own display keeps its bank, and presses from the screen are marked "on screen". (How it works, in detail →)

The tones aren't software: they're community captures on TONE3000, free with a free account. The hardware isn't free either: an FCB1010, a Scarlett and a guitar.

What it can't do (yet)

  • Played, not A/B'd. I haven't compared each era against the records yet.
  • The harmony is a diatonic third, one key per song. Sixths and key changes still need a second player.
  • Xruns aren't fully gone. Real-time priority needs a re-login I haven't done; until then, close the tuner before you play.
  • The pedal can't follow the screen, and GuitarMood is a 76 MB download to draw one window. Which brings me to what's next.

Next: a much lighter Omarchy plugin

The tuner showed the better shape. Fretwise is a bar widget and a QML panel with a tiny standard-library Python process behind it, installed with one command. GuitarMood should be the same: the bank and scene in the bar, the board in a panel drawn by the shell that's already running, and a listing in the Omarchy marketplace. Then turning the rig on is clicking an icon in the bar. Let's make Omarchy the best OS for musicians.

Try it

Everything is on main of github.com/saada/fcb1010-biasfx2 (MIT). On Omarchy:

sudo pacman -S --needed uv qtractor guitarix \
  x42-plugins-lv2 lsp-plugins-lv2
omarchy plugin add \
  https://github.com/WayneKruger/omarchy-fretwise.git
git clone \
  https://github.com/saada/fcb1010-biasfx2
cd fcb1010-biasfx2
./bootstrap.sh ~/Downloads/TONE3000-*.tar.gz
uv run rig.py send   # the FCB layout, once
uv run --project guitarmood \
  guitarmood install

First set your audio interface and MIDI port at the top of tone3000.py, turn your monitor knob down, and grab the TONE3000 download from tone3000.com/plugin. The README has the details.

Then every time you play: SUPER+SPACE → GuitarMood. SW10 to tune. Pick a bank with UP/DOWN and press RHYTHM, CLEAN or ACOUSTIC to load the era. Play. SUPER+W when you're done. The one-time setup (FCB upload, real time) and the Maiden song recipes are in the README's practice guide.

Last time I pair-programmed with Claude Fable. This time, all of it, from the reverse-engineered preset format to GuitarMood and this write-up, I built with Claude Code running Claude Opus 5.5. Claude wrote the code and ran the research agents and every measurement. I played, listened, and said "bro, wtf is this ugly tuner" when it was needed.
